Guide

How Tracklock matches your Valorant sensitivity, and how to get the most out of each drill.

Quick start

  1. Type your Valorant sensitivity and mouse DPI in the launch panel. Anything from 0.0001 to 100 works.
  2. Pick a scenario, a difficulty and a run length.
  3. Press Start. The mouse locks to the game. Keep your crosshair on the target until the timer runs out.
  4. Esc pauses, R restarts, and Enter plays again from the results screen.

How sensitivity matching works

At sensitivity 1.0, Valorant turns 0.07° for every count your mouse reports. Tracklock uses the same formula, so the same number gives the same turn:

degrees per count = sensitivity × 0.07

cm/360 = 360 ÷ (sensitivity × 0.07) ÷ DPI × 2.54

For example, 0.4 at 800 DPI is 40.8 cm for a full turn in both games. The horizontal field of view defaults to Valorant’s 103°, so targets look the same size at the same distance.

Sensitivity accepts up to 4 decimals. The slider is logarithmic: each sixth of the track is one power of ten, from 0.0001 to 100. Use ↑/↓ in the number box for fine steps. Add Shift for steps ten times bigger.

Getting 1:1 mouse input

  • Use Chrome or Edge on Windows or macOS and leave Raw input on. The browser then reports raw mouse counts, like Valorant does, and ignores Windows pointer speed and “Enhance pointer precision”.
  • Firefox, Safari and Linux browsers don’t offer raw input yet. Set the Windows pointer speed to the default (6 of 11) and turn off Enhance pointer precision. On macOS, turn off pointer acceleration in System Settings → Mouse.
  • The in-game corner readout shows whether raw input is active.

Verify it

Open the Sensitivity check room. Line your crosshair up with the 0° marker, then move your mouse exactly your cm/360 along a ruler. You should land back on 0°. You can also leave Input multiplier at 1, move the mouse 10 cm and read Mouse moved. If it shows a different distance X, set the multiplier (Settings → Sensitivity) to 10 ÷ X.

Scenarios

Scoring

Accuracy
Time on target ÷ time spent firing. With Always firing on, that is the whole run.
Score
100 points per second on target. Time on the strafe bot’s head is worth 200, time on its legs 85. The score does not depend on the weapon.
Hits and damage
Your weapon fires at its Valorant rate while you hold the trigger. Every shot is a hit or a miss, and hits add the weapon’s close-range damage: 160 for a Vandal headshot, 40 for a body shot.
Longest streak
The longest unbroken time you stayed on target.
Avg recovery
How long it takes you to get back on the target after losing it for more than 0.1 s.
Personal best
Kept separately for each scenario, difficulty and run length, so you only compare like with like.

Weapons

Pick a weapon in the launch panel. Fire rates and damage follow Valorant’s close-range numbers; the hitboxes on the bot are head, body (with arms) and legs.

Hold the left mouse button in the sensitivity check room to test-fire without scoring.

Tracking tips

  • Aim for smoothness over speed. Match the target’s speed instead of chasing it.
  • Loosen your grip. Tense muscles cause jitter, which shows up as a low streak.
  • Stay on one sensitivity for weeks. Consistency beats constant tweaking.
  • Mix drills. Smooth and Strafe build control. Reactive and Micro build fast corrections.
  • Watch the accuracy-over-time chart. A drop late in the run means focus or fatigue.

Privacy

No accounts and no tracking. Settings and stats stay in this browser’s local storage.
